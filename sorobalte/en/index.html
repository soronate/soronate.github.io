Riga, the Baltic capital

Founded in 1201, Riga is the largest city in the Baltic states. Its old town has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1997, with medieval lanes, guild halls and church spires.

It also has one of Europe’s finest Art Nouveau districts, around Alberta Street, and a vast market housed in former zeppelin hangars.

Discover Latvia

The country is small, flat and green: two hours from Riga, the scenery changes completely.

Gauja and Sigulda

Sandstone cliffs, medieval castles and trails in the country’s oldest national park.

Jūrmala coast

A long fine-sand beach and 19th-century wooden villas, 30 minutes from Riga.

Kuldīga

Old town on the UNESCO list since 2023 and the Venta waterfall, Europe’s widest at about 250 m.

Riga, the historic heart

Morning in the Old Town (Town Hall Square, cathedral, Swedish Gate). Lunch at the Central Market, then Alberta Street and its Art Nouveau façades.

Train to Majori (about 30 minutes), the beach, pedestrian Jomas iela and wooden villas.

Food and traditions

Rye bread

Rupjmaize, dark and dense, goes with almost every meal. It also makes a dessert, rupjmaizes kārtojums (pictured).

Grey peas with bacon

A traditional winter dish served with onion and kefir.

Jāņi, Midsummer

June 23–24: flower crowns, bonfires, songs and caraway cheese.

Rīgas Melnais balzams

A dark, bitter herbal liqueur made in Riga since 1752, drunk as a digestif or in coffee.

Smoked fish

Smoked herring, sprats and eel, to taste at the Central Market with rye bread.

Dziesmu svētki, the song festival

Since 1873, thousands of singers and dancers have gathered roughly every five years. The tradition is recognised by UNESCO; around 500,000 visitors were expected in 2018. The great stage at Mežaparks in Riga has hosted it since 1955.

Frequently asked questions

When should I go?

May to September for beaches and long days. Midsummer (Jāņi) is on June 23–24. Winters are cold, with lovely Christmas markets in Riga.

What currency and entry rules?

Latvia uses the euro and is part of the Schengen area: EU citizens can travel with an ID card.

Which languages are spoken?

Latvian is the official language. English is common in tourism and Russian is widely spoken.

How do I get from the airport to the centre?

Riga airport is about 10 km from the centre. A bus, taxi or ride-hailing car reaches the Old Town in 20 to 30 minutes.

Can I manage without a car?

Yes for Riga, which is easy on foot. Trains link Riga with Jūrmala and Sigulda; a car is still useful for the countryside and the west coast.
